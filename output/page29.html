Goth aesthetic gone wild: a user's self-inflicted black screen of non-death in Windows 3.1’s

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Microsoft senior software engineer Raymond Chen has explained what Windows is doing when you hold down the power button, and shared a story about the time a customer accidentally created a Goth Mode for Windows 3.1.

There's a section in The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy (actually, page 116 of this writer's dog-eared copy of The Restaurant at the End of the Universe) in which Zaphod Beeblebrox observes of a ship's interior: "Every time you operate one of these weird black controls that are labeled in black on black background, a little black light lights up black to let you know you've done it."

The same turned out to be true of a user who called Microsoft customer support to complain that after Windows 3.1 booted up, all that remained after the product logo disappeared was a blank screen.

It turned out the customer liked black a little too much and, according to Chen, made themself a Goth theme.

The customer support engineer on Microsoft's end pieced it together. The customer had, according to Chen, "set the wallpaper color to black. They set the title bar color to black. They set the button color to black. They set the window background color to black. They set the window text color to black.

"Every possible color you could pick in the Colors control panel was set to black."

We suspect the mouse pointer, at least, would have stuck around, unless the customer had also managed to change some settings around this. And then there are the icons… However, Windows 3.1 was a long time ago, and this writer's attempts at theming then were the consequences of growing up in the '70s and '80s, which resulted in some interesting neon, orange, and brown combinations.

As for Goth mode, Chen said, "Once you get yourself into this situation, it’s hard to get out because you can't see anything." One solution he suggested was deleting the [colors] section from the win.ini file.

Chen told the anecdote as a coda to the question – what exactly is modern Windows doing when you hold down the power button on a PC? According to Chen, after eight seconds of holding the power button, the Windows kernel realizes a forced power-off is happening and tries to take a kernel dump for diagnostics.

He explained that the kernel dumps were classified by Microsoft as "long power button hold" (LPBH) failures, with one of the bits of information captured indicating if all the pixels on the screen were black. The video driver might therefore be the culprit. Or whatever was responsible for drawing to the screen.

Today's Windows struggles with dark mode. But in the dim, distant past, every pixel being black might mean the user was either Hotblack Desiato or had done something very silly indeed. ®

Harry Styles has recreated his original X Factor audition, performing Stevie Wonder‘s ‘Isn’t She Lovely’ while dressed as his 16-year-old self – watch footage below.

The singer was performing the first of two ‘Harryween’ concerts at Madison Square Garden last night (October 30), marking the penultimate date of his mammoth 30-night residency at the venue.

For the occasion, Styles initially appeared dressed as Elvis Presley, wearing the King’s signature white jumpsuit, complete with a cape and gold sunglasses. His backing band and dancers also embraced the Halloween theme, dressing as various versions of Styles from throughout his career, including looks inspired by his appearances at Coachella, the Grammys and the Met Gala.

Later in the show, however, Styles changed into an altogether different costume, recreating the clothes he wore for his original audition on The X Factor in 2010.

He went on to perform ‘Isn’t She Lovely’, the Stevie Wonder classic that helped launch his career when he sang it for judges Simon Cowell, Nicole Scherzinger and Louis Walsh at the age of 16. Styles also jokingly recreated one of his memorable comments from the audition, telling the Madison Square Garden audience: “I work in a bakery.”

The original audition saw Styles arrive as a teenager from Holmes Chapel, Cheshire, where he had been working part-time at a bakery.

Although viewers initially saw him perform Wonder’s 1976 hit a cappella, extended footage released by The X Factor in 2022 revealed that he had first attempted Train’s ‘Hey, Soul Sister’.

Cowell interrupted that performance and asked: “I don’t know whether it’s the track that’s throwing you, but can I hear something just you, without any music?”

Styles then sang ‘Isn’t She Lovely’, advancing to the next stage of the competition before eventually being brought together with Niall Horan, Zayn Malik, Liam Payne and Louis Tomlinson to form One Direction.

The costume changes were part of Styles’ revived ‘Harryween’ tradition, which first saw him hold two special Halloween concerts at Madison Square Garden in 2021.

On the opening night that year, he dressed as Dorothy from The Wizard Of Oz and performed ‘Somewhere Over The Rainbow’, with his band dressed as other characters from the 1939 film.

The following evening, he wore an elaborate clown costume and covered Britney Spears‘ ‘Toxic’.

Styles continued the tradition in Los Angeles in 2022, when he dressed as Danny Zuko from Grease and covered Olivia Newton-John‘s ‘Hopelessly Devoted To You’.

The latest concert comes towards the end of Styles’ 30-night Madison Square Garden residency, which began in August as part of his ongoing ‘Together, Together’ world tour.

Earlier this week, he performed ‘Ever Since New York’ for the first time in four years, while the residency has also seen him pay tribute to his late One Direction bandmate Liam Payne with the new song ‘What Is Enough’.

The New York run concludes tonight (October 31) with a second ‘Harryween’ show, before Styles heads to Australia for concerts in Melbourne and Sydney in November and December.

The tour comes in support of his fourth solo album ‘Kiss All The Time. Disco, Occasionally’, which was released in March and received a four-star review from NME.

Reviewing the ‘Together, Together’ tour in Amsterdam in May, NME awarded the performance five stars, praising its sense of community and describing it as “a hopeful celebration of community and connection”.

Styles has also announced further tour dates for 2027, including additional Wembley Stadium concerts and support from Kylie Minogue, LCD Soundsystem and more.

Seabirds racing home over the ocean can get jet-lagged

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

Flying across several time zones often causes jet lag, a feeling when the body insists it's the middle of the night while the clock says it's lunchtime. Now it turns out this nuisance is not exclusive to humans.

A new Science study on Manx shearwaters, seabirds that can fly hundreds of kilometers a day over the North Atlantic, shows they jet-lag themselves during their excursions. And because these birds navigate by the Sun, that jet lag leaves a measurable mark—it skews their compass.

Clockwork compass

Patrick Lewin is a behavioral and sensory ecologist at the University of Oxford and lead author of the study. “Manx shearwaters are medium-sized birds, about the same size as a pigeon,” Lewin said. “They are white underneath and black on top, but they have very long wings, and they are closely related to albatross.” Manx shearwaters nest in burrows on coastal islands around the North Atlantic. Most breed around the UK and Ireland, with about half of their population living in Wales.

The FDA wants to pull its approval of a drug. It’s not so easy.

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

In May 2021, the US Food and Drug Administration released a lengthy briefing document on an experimental medication for a rare autoimmune form of vasculitis that often damages the kidneys and lungs. The agency was on the fence about whether to approve the treatment, and it wanted an independent advisory committee to weigh in. Among the questions at hand: Was the drug meaningfully better than existing therapies?

Participants in the study, which had been conducted by the California biotechnology company ChemoCentryx, did appear to have slightly higher rates of symptom remission after one year. But according to the FDA, it was hard to interpret the results because the study’s design had been less than optimal. Some advisory committee members expressed a similar view. The effect of the drug, said one, “is just not well verified by this study.”

At the same time, many patients, and some physicians, saw in the data a potentially helpful medication, one that might lessen patients’ reliance on steroids, which are highly effective but often cause severe side effects.
