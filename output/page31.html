Review: The new Kindle Colorsoft is... fine.

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The best Kindle e-readers are essential for book lovers, and Amazon just released a new Kindle Colorsoft (and the Kindle Colorsoft Signature Edition with an aluminum body), along with a new basic Kindle, Kindle Paperwhite/Paperwhite Signature Edition, and the Kindle Click page-turner. Following last year's refresh of the Kindle Scribe lineup, that means Amazon has an all-new Kindle family, just in time for the holidays.

I've been reviewing the Kindle Colorsoft Signature Edition for Mashable for the past few weeks. Except for the new aluminum body, fig colorway, and higher storage, it's functionally the same as the base Colorsoft model, so we won't be reviewing them separately.

If you've been curious about Amazon's color e-readers, then I'd recommend reading my Kindle Colorsoft review first. Unfortunately, while there's a lot that I do love about this device, it doesn't fix the biggest problem — the color is still very disappointing.

That cover is definitely yellow-ish. Credit: Timothy Werth / Mashable

Kindle Colorsoft review: Manage your expectations when it comes to color

Now, I want to be clear about one thing: Amazon uses E Ink technology for its e-readers. This distinctive, paper-like digital display is a product of the E Ink Corporation. And the truth is that E Ink just doesn't do color very well. So, the technology is what it is.

The E Ink color experience on the Kindle Colorsoft is disappointing. That was true with the original model from 2024, and it hasn't changed with the 2026 model. What has changed is that the Colorsoft now offers true edge-to-edge color, so there are no dimming or lighting issues around the edges.

But Amazon promises readers something more: "Paper-like color perfect for comics, graphic novels, and more." And that's not what I'm seeing. In fact, some of Amazon's official photography with the Kindle Colorsoft appears to exaggerate the color saturation, at least to my eye. I've never seen color quite that bright in practice.

I haven't seen colors quite this bright on my review unit, sadly. Credit: Amazon

I put the Kindle side-by-side with illustrated books, comic books, and graphic novels I have on my bookshelf, and the difference is very obvious, even when you crank up the vividness and brightness to the limit. There's just no comparison, really.

Even in comics with more neutral tones and softer colors like the classic Batman comic The Long Halloween, the difference stands out clearly. How important is the color problem? Well, the word "color" is right there in the name — Kindle Colorsoft. Though, I suppose the emphasis should be on "soft."

The color difference is obvious. Credit: Timothy Werth / Mashable What elder millennial nerd doesn't own a copy of 'Watchmen'? Credit: Timothy Werth / Mashable

I have seen more vibrant and colorful electronic paper technology. Samsung's ePaper does a better job with color, though there are other trade-offs. It takes time for the screen to change to a new image, which isn't an option in an e-reader that demands near-instant page turns.

In my photos of Samsung's ePaper, you'll notice brighter colors, but there's a reason Samsung reserves this tech for commercial use.

Samsung's ePaper commercial display technology on display. Credit: Timothy Werth / Mashable A closer look at Samsung's ePaper commercial display technology. Credit: Timothy Werth / Mashable

If you want to read comics, magazines, or illustrated books in digital form, a tablet like the new iPad Mini or Amazon Alexa tablets would be a better option. (Mashable is in the process of reviewing both of those devices.)

And you can always go to an actual bookstore.

Amazon Kindle Colorsoft Signature Edition: What I liked

To be clear, there is a lot to love about the Kindle Colorsoft, particularly the Kindle Colorsoft Signature Edition. I love the fig color, which is a close match for the new burgundy iPhone 18 Pro. (Fun fact: An Amazon spokesperson told Mashable the fig color was inspired by the same industry trend report that inspired the burgundy iPhone.)

The new aluminum body available in the Paperwhite SE and Colorsoft SE is worth the upgrade. They have a similar feel to the Apple iPad Air, which I also loved. It's also satisfying to see the cover screen artwork and your book covers in color.

Credit: Timothy Werth / Mashable Credit: Timothy Werth / Mashable

I also appreciated the ability to zoom in on comic panels and illustrations, which is a great way to appreciate fine details in the artwork.

Credit: Timothy Werth / Mashable ...enhance! Credit: Timothy Werth / Mashable

You can double-click to read comics panel-by-panel on the Colorsoft, rather than page-by-page. Often, this works really well, but for artists who use complicated panels of varying sizes and styles (like you'll find in The Long Halloween), some of the panels fit very awkwardly on the screen.

The new Kindle Colorsoft also has a lot of the features we loved in other Kindle devices — a waterproof design, a battery that lasts for weeks, auto-adjusting light. Amazon also moved the power button to the side, which is a smart update. Only the Signature Edition supports wireless charging, and it now has magnetic pogo pins on the back. That means if you're used to using Qi wireless charging accessories with your Kindle, they won't work here. For me, this isn't a problem.

To sum up — the Kindle Colorsoft isn't a bad color e-reader. It just suffers from the same problems all color e-readers do.

The Kindle Colorsoft: Who it's for

If you believe some color is better than no color, or if you're a note-taker who loves to highlight their books, then the Kindle Colorsoft would be a smart upgrade from the basic Kindle. (Pro tip: We named the Kindle Scribe Colorsoft a Mashable Choice product, and the ability to write on the screen lets you use color in much better ways.) However, the Kobo Libra Colour is retaining its top spot as our favorite color e-reader.

The Kindle Colorsoft would also be a smart option for kids in the process of graduating from illustrated kids' books to chapter books. The Colorsoft could help make this transition a bit more inviting, and parents can also get a Kindle Colorsoft Kids Edition for $299.99.

This Martian meteorite just got dubbed a Rosetta Stone. Heres why.

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

One meteorite from Mars adds weight to the expression "no stone left unturned." 

Yang Liu, a geologist at NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory, almost didn't heed that wisdom when she nearly declined the chance to study the rock. It looked like the most common type of Martian meteorite found on Earth. But something about its texture and minerals made her take a look.

In hindsight, Liu is glad she did. The space rock, known as Teghaza 001, appears to be a piece of Mars that formed more than 4.1 billion years ago, making it one of the oldest Martian meteorites scientists have ever seen.

"This meteorite is really unique," said Liu, the lead author of the findings, in a statement. "One of the paper's reviewers called it a Rosetta Stone for Mars."

The meteorite, found in the Sahara Desert in 2022, preserves a snapshot of Mars as it looked more than 4 billion years ago, perhaps less than 500 million years after the planet formed. Even then, the water locked inside its minerals had already lost much of its lighter hydrogen to space. That chemical fingerprint shows the Red Planet began losing its water almost from the word "go." The discovery provides context for any debate about life on early Mars: Whatever biology that might have existed, even early on, probably faced a world already drying out.

SEE ALSO: NASA's Voyager 1 gives mission control another moment of panic

Every day, about 48.5 tons of space rock and dust strike Earth's atmosphere, according to NASA. Most of it burns up on the way down, and much of what survives sinks into the oceans, which cover more than 70 percent of the planet. People have found more than 82,000 meteorites, but only about half of one percent of them came from Mars.

Nearly every known Martian meteorite formed from magma, or molten rock, and most of them belong to three main families: shergottites, nakhlites, and chassignites. All three formed less than 2.4 billion years ago. 

A close-up look at a slice extracted from the Martian meteorite, Teghaza 001. Credit: NASA / JPL-Caltech

The nakhlites and chassignites seem closely related through a process called fractional crystallization. As magma cools, certain minerals harden first and separate out, which changes the chemistry of the remaining liquid. Scientists think both groups formed in the same volcanic system.

But Teghaza 001 doesn't fit any of these groups. Its minerals and chemistry set it apart from every other Martian meteorite. The study appears in the journal Science.

"This is really our first piece of Martian crust from a part of Mars we've never had a sample of before," said Lee Saper, a co-author now based at NASA's Ames Research Center in California.

Clues in the rock's minerals point to a busy water cycle on early Mars, with water moving between rock, ground, and air. The minerals reveal two sources: water that rose from deep inside the planet, and surface water that had already lost much of its lighter hydrogen to space. 

Scientists use geological clues to piece together ancient Mars' past water history. Credit: NASA illustration

Geologists can tell the two apart because hydrogen comes in a normal form and a heavier form called deuterium. Because the lighter form escapes into space more easily, any water left behind gets richer in deuterium over time. The surface water in Teghaza 001 carries extra deuterium.

From about 4.1 to 3.7 billion years ago, Mars had a fairly dry climate, but one that could have still allowed liquid water on the surface at times, possibly enabling life. Scientists believe the Red Planet's surface eventually dried out to roughly today's barren state less than 3 billion years ago.

Thanks to Mars' lack of shifting tectonic plates, its ground maintains a record of more than 4 billion years of history. That allows scientists to estimate the ages of different parts of the planet's surface by counting craters: the older the region, the more battle scars it should have. 

Based on the specimen's minerals and chemistry, scientists trace the rock to one of a handful of locations in Mars' southern highlands. Credit: NASA / JPL-Caltech / USGS

The mix of minerals matches what NASA's Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter has seen in the heavily pocked southern highlands of Mars. Scientists pinpointed 13 places on Mars whose minerals and chemistry are consistent with the meteorite.

To date Teghaza 001, scientists used several methods, including one cutting-edge technique that tracks radioactive rubidium, said Francois Tissot, a Caltech scientist and co-author. The rubidium slowly transforms into strontium at a steady rate; thus, the amount of the latter within a sample stands in for a clock. 

"Our hope is to pave the way for dating even more Martian meteorites," Tissot said in a statement, "or samples returned from other planets, their moons, and asteroids."

Backbone of a beast – charting Nvidia's rise to networking titan

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Nvidia is more than just a leader in graphics and AI hardware. It has also become a titan of the networking industry with bigger datacenter Ethernet sales than any other vendor.

In Q2, Nvidia's networking revenues topped $17.5 billion. To put that in perspective, Cisco's revenue during that same period, across all products, was $17.3 billion.

GPUzilla's arrival as a networking power feels sudden, yet Jensen Huang started working towards this status in 2019 with the $6.9 billion acquisition of long-time networking partner Mellanox .

At the time, Mellanox wasn't a big name in Ethernet switching. The company was mostly known for its ConnectX NICs and InfiniBand switches. Outside of high-performance computing (HPC) and supercomputing clusters, and a big deployment at Microsoft, InfiniBand wasn't widely deployed in hyperscale datacenters.

Buying Mellanox allowed Nvidia to reposition itself as a system builder, which means something different in the context of distributed compute. Nvidia understood its datacenter customers weren't interested in buying GPUs or even servers; they wanted clusters of systems. For that, Nvidia needed interconnect and Mellanox's InfiniBand fit the bill.

Relentless co-design

Over the years, Huang has talked about co-design – designing components and software to work together for specific set of jobs rather than to be the best GPU, switch, or NIC. Its work with Mellanox is a prime example of that approach.

According to Dell'Oro Group analyst Sameh Boujelbene, Nvidia's decision to integrate compute and networking became key to the company's success.

"Customers choose Nvidia for its tightly integrated compute and networking architecture, optimized for AI workloads," she told The Register. That integration makes deployments easier and speeds time to market.

Ben Mabey, CTO of pioneering AI and machine learning company Recursion, told The Reg Nvidia's integration schemes won him over.

Founded in 2013, nearly a decade before ChatGPT made AI the subject of dinner-table discussion, Recursion sought to apply machine learning running atop GPUs to the problem of drug discovery.

This work involved churning through massive datasets which, Mabey tells us, now total more than 50 petabytes in size.

"When you think about our workloads, it makes LLMs workload training seem easy compared to the amount of data that has to be moved around," he told The Register.

"In the biological space and sciences in general, it's not well appreciated just how big the data actually is," he added. "The internet's big, but in the grand scheme of things, the text that people are training on is actually pretty small, versus the petabytes of data that we have of these images."

Recursion's first cluster was a system called BioHive Zero which it cobbled together from GTX 1080 graphics cards originally intended for gaming systems. It used Ethernet to connect GPUs and ran into the limits of conventional networking.

So, for its second cluster, an A100-based machine with around 300 accelerators, Recursion went with Nvidia's Quantum InfiniBand switches and ConnectX NICs.

As Mabey explains it, buying its compute and interconnects from the same vendor vastly simplified optimization and troubleshooting. When Recursion reached out to Nvidia for help tuning the system, they were able to consider it as a single system, rather than discrete compute and networking resources. This level of integration had only previously been offered by boutique supercomputer builders like Cray or Atos (Eviden/Bull/whatever it gets renamed next).

A wider Spectrum

Nvidia hasn't always listed networking revenue as a single line item in its financials. However, based on CFO commentary and a little back of the napkin math we can fill in the blanks.

Nvidia's networking revenues have grown steadily since the Mellanox acquisition. At two moments, its revenue shot up suddenly.

The first came in the 2023 calendar year (FY24) after OpenAI kicked off the AI gold rush with the launch of ChatGPT.

The second spread across 2024 and 2025, when Nvidia started selling Blackwell-generation GPUs and then started to ship them in large quantities.

"I see two major inflection points, both coinciding with the Blackwell platform ramp: the introduction of NVLink switches and the accelerating adoption of Spectrum-X Ethernet switches for AI back-end networks, driven by growing deployments of Nvidia GPU clusters and increased attach rates of Nvidia switches, NICs, optics, and cables," Boujelbene said.

It was around this time that users started deploy to Ethernet switches in large volume across Nvidia clusters. Elon Musk's Colossus supercomputer was one of the earliest examples.

As we reported at the time, the first half of the 200,000-chip H100 and H200-based cluster came online in 2024 and, at the time, was among the largest ever clusters deployed using Nvidia's Spectrum-X Ethernet switches.

Meta has also made extensive use of Nvidia's Spectrum switches in its own training clusters, including at its million-GPU Prometheus site in New Albany, Ohio.

There are a couple reasons Nvidia's Spectrum-X offering is doing so well.

First, through a combination of Spectrum Ethernet switches, superNICs, and some clever software optimizations, Nvidia was able to replicate many of the qualities that made InfiniBand so attractive while also benefiting from significantly higher radix and interoperability.

Ethernet switches, including Nvidia's own, have historically offered more ports. The higher the radix, the fewer switches are necessary to support a cluster at a given port speed.

This often gets misconstrued to mean that InfiniBand can't scale beyond clusters of a certain size, which isn't necessarily the case. But for clusters exceeding a certain number of GPUs, Ethernet is often more economical because it requires less equipment while achieving similar utilization.

That said, Nvidia's Spectrum-X isn't exactly unique. Broadcom, Cisco, AMD and others have managed to achieve utilization similar to InfiniBand through various investments in smartNICs and switches.

Nvidia's main differentiator, as Mabey and Boujelbene noted earlier, is the level of integration between the compute and networking.

The next frontier

Then Blackwell hit the market, enabling much greater scale and creating a surge in Nvidia's networking revenue.

Prior to Blackwell, Nvidia's NVLink and NVSwitch interconnect tech had largely been limited to single systems with up to eight GPUs. With the launch of the GB200 NVL72, these NVSwitches were broken out into dedicated appliances. (And yes, we remember GH200 NVL256 and NVL32, but from what we can tell these were never shipped in any meaningful volume, if at all.)

For every NVL72 rack system sold, Nvidia also sold nine NVSwitch trays, which enabled the system's six dozen GPUs to function as a single enormous GPU. This effectively guaranteed steady networking revenue growth: even if customers didn't buy Nvidia's scale-out switches, if they wanted NVL72 racks, they were automatically buying NVSwitch.

"We estimate that NVSwitch now accounts for roughly one-third of Nvidia’s networking revenue, up from virtually zero less than two years ago," Boujelbene told El Reg. "This underscores how quickly NVLink switching has become a major growth engine for Nvidia’s networking business."

Nvidia is now moving to grow its networking business even further with a new licensing program.

Until recently, Nvidia's NVSwitch tech was limited to its own accelerators. However, with the introduction of NVLink Fusion early last year, Nvidia opened the interconnect tech up to third parties to license.

Many chip designers, including Marvell, Qualcomm, Arm, Fujitsu, MediaTek, and d-Matrix, have announced plans to develop CPUs and XPUs using NVLink Fusion. The adoption of the interconnect opens the door to additional NVSwitch sales, as the chips using NVLink Fusion still rely on them for chip-to-chip communications, and there's no other place to get them, as Nvidia has yet to license out NVSwitch.

"NVLink Fusion allows Nvidia to extend its networking footprint beyond its own GPUs into the rapidly growing custom accelerator market. I see it as a strategic move to capture a larger share of AI networking spending, even in clusters that do not use Nvidia GPUs," Boujelbene said.

Nvidia's networking ambitions don't stop there.

"Nvidia continues to pursue opportunities in emerging networking domains as network requirements evolve to keep pace with increasingly demanding AI workloads," Boujelbene added. "The latest example is scale-in networking, designed to address the new requirements of agentic AI, while innovations such as CPO will help overcome growing bandwidth, power, and scalability challenges." ®
