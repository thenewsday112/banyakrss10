U2 Announces 2027 U.K. and European Stadium Dates for ‘The Greatest Show On Earth’ World Tour

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Step right up, step right up: U2 is bringing The Greatest Show On Earth tour to the U.K. and Europe in 2027, announcing on Thursday (Oct. 29) a new run of stadium dates spanning April through July. 

Billed as Carnaval De Luz Presents: The Greatest Show On Earth, the leg will launch Apr. 26 at Stockholm’s Strawberry Arena, with British rockers Wolf Alice serving as support. The itinerary includes stops in Paris, Lisbon, Amsterdam, London, Berlin and Milan, culminating with two nights at Milan’s Stadio San Siro on July 5 and 6. 

U2 will also play two shows at London’s Tottenham Hotspur Stadium on June 18 and 19. As per a press release, Spanish dates — and a further North American trek — are expected to be announced soon.

The announcement follows a sellout of initial Latin American shows, which begin Feb. 23 at Mexico City’s Estadio GNP Seguros before continuing to Brazil and Argentina. Support for this portion of the tour will come from Beck.

Tickets for the U.K. and European dates will first be available through the U2.com subscriber presale beginning Monday (Nov. 2), at 10 a.m. local time. Visa cardholders will have access to a separate presale on Wednesday (Nov. 4), from 10 a.m. to 10 p.m. local time. Further ticketing information can be found at the band’s official website.

In a new press statement attributed to band members Bono, The Edge, Adam Clayton and Larry Mullen, the band said: “‘The Greatest Show on Earth’? Now that’s a throwdown. It’s a dare, to ourselves as much as anyone, to try and have the best night of anybody’s life.

“A bit of showman, a bit of shaman, a riot of colour, unreasonable guitar, bass and drums, and tunes you can hold onto. Because if people are gonna show up, we certainly are.”

The Greatest Show On Earth tour follows U2’s acclaimed residency at The Sphere in Las Vegas, where the group played 40 dates across six months from September and reimagined 1991 album Achtung Baby through an immersive, high-technologu production. 

Reporting from opening night, Billboard’s Katie Atkinson described the four-piece as “exactly the right band to welcome the mind-blowing space,” adding that the show was “magic” and “U2’s music and message always remained the focal point throughout the two-hour set – even when a pop-culture kaleidoscope of images scrolled up the 366-foot screen in rapid succession.”

Last month, U2 marked their 50th anniversary as a group with a lunchtime performance for students and teachers at Mount Temple Comprehensive, the Dublin school attended by the members, followed by a surprise performance on the city’s Grafton Street.

The band have also confirmed that plans for their new show to come to Ireland in 2028 are in the works. “We can’t say too much about the next leg of The Greatest Show On Earth – we’re still figuring it out – but what we can say is, we’ll be wrapping it up in Ireland,” said The Edge. “There’s nowhere else we’d want to be for that party.”

Before hitting stadiums, U2 will go very small next week for an exclusive, intimate gig for BBC Music on Tuesday (Nov. 3) at an as-yet-unannounced venue in central London for a select group of fans.

U2 stand as one of the most successful rock acts on the Billboard 200, with eight No. 1 albums on the chart, beginning with The Joshua Tree in 1987. On the Billboard Hot 100, meanwhile, the band reached the top 10 with several singles, including “With Or Without You,” which topped the chart for three weeks in 1987 and “I Still Haven’t Found What I’m Looking For,” which also reached No. 1 that year.

Yungblud Exhibition Featuring Previously Unseen ‘Idols’ Photos to Open in London

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Yungblud is inviting fans behind the scenes of his blockbuster Idols era with a new exhibition in London, featuring previously unseen images captured by his photographer and creative director, Tom Pallant.

Organized by Behind The Gallery, Yungblud: Idols will open on Nov. 20 at Union Pacific, located at the gallery’s 17 Goulston Street venue. The exhibition is free to attend and marks the collection’s exclusive U.K. showcase following its world debut in Sydney earlier this year.

Pallant has worked with Yungblud since 2019, and his images have documented the British rock singer’s rise through multiple international tours and major festival appearances including Glastonbury and Coachella. The new exhibition captures the creative process of Idols, Yungblud’s latest studio album, which was released last summer and hit the summit of the Official U.K. Albums Chart, marking the 29-year-old’s fourth No. 1 record in his home nation.

The exhibition will also feature two special events across its opening weekend. An album playback is scheduled for Nov. 20, followed by an Q&A with Pallant the next day. Further details about the events will be announced in due course.

Visitors will also have the opportunity to purchase photographs from the collection. Each print will be hand signed by Pallant and produced on fine-art paper.

In a statement, Pallant said: “Being able to bring this show to London and share the Idols world at home is going to be super special. As we close the chapter on Idols, this feels like the right moment to bring together a collection of our favourite work, including a lot of images that both we and others might have missed along the way.

“This exhibition builds on the foundation we made in Sydney. I’ve always been drawn to the moments that happen around a performance as much as the performance itself, the exhaustion, the chaos, the feeling of putting everything you have into something. I’m hoping people are able to view Idols as a whole project and see the journey we all went on.”

Stephen Dallimore, creative director of Behind The Gallery, added: “Tom Pallant’s photography uniquely captures the raw emotional energy of Yungblud, an artist who has taken the world by storm, and it feels only right that U.K. fans now get to be part of that story. It’s very rare that one photographer can tell an artist’s world like Tom can with Yungblud. Over the course of the exhibition, we’ll celebrate with fans these powerful images through photography, music and storytelling.”

Pallant has previously released a photography book titled All My Friends Have Deserted Me, which chronicled Yungblud’s journey through to his sold-out show at London’s Alexandra Palace in 2021. He has also won three Abbey Road Music Photography Awards over the years.

Yungblud featured on the cover of Billboard U.K. last December, shortly after the arrival of One More Time, a joint EP with Aerosmith. The project marked his highest-charting release on the Billboard 200 to date, landing at No. 9.

Scientists think theyve just discovered a new dwarf planet like Pluto

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Astronomers have just spotted a small, bright world in the outer reaches of the solar system they think could be a dwarf planet like Pluto. 

The mysterious space object, for now known as 2026 ED138, lies about 46 times farther from the sun than Earth, and may measure between 370 to 930 miles wide. Scientists estimate it's about 1.1 billion miles farther than Pluto in the Kuiper Belt, a zone of icy debris past Neptune.

Matthew Holman, a researcher at the Harvard & Smithsonian Center for Astrophysics, made the discovery two weeks ago by running data from the new Vera C. Rubin Observatory through a program he built with fellow astronomer Kevin Napier. 

"My first reaction was one of excited disbelief," Holman said in a statement. "Objects like this are very rare, so I immediately started checking whether I or the code had done something wrong."

The International Astronomical Union's Minor Planet Center, the official recordkeeper for new objects in our solar system, announced it as a candidate on Wednesday. If confirmed, ED138 could become the sixth officially recognized dwarf planet in the solar system, following Ceres, Pluto, Haumea, Makemake, and Eris.

Rubin's detection of a previously unknown world in our own cosmic backyard highlights a new accelerated way of doing astronomy. After the initial find, the team was able to piece together a detailed "picture" of the object in three days. By pairing the observatory's repeated sky scans with archival data and pictures from other telescopes, the researchers learned quite a bit about ED138 and its stretched orbit around the sun. 

You can think of a dwarf planet as a scaled-down planet. It gets pulled around the sun by its own gravity, but it's not quite massive enough to clear other space rocks out of its way like a regular planet does. Pluto's 2006 demotion from planet status occurred because other icy bodies cross its orbit. 

"Once we identify a new object, we can search back months, and sometimes even decades, finding it in images where nobody knew to look," said Mario Jurić, Rubin's lead solar system scientist at the University of Washington, in a statement. "This ability to both discover and 'precover' objects is transforming how quickly and accurately we can determine their orbits and understand the distant solar system."

Astronomers say ED138 shines so brightly that folks with fairly small telescopes could probably see it. The reason no one has noticed it until now may be that it hides in plain sight among the Milky Way's crowded band of stars in the sky.

Looking back in the vault, Rubin's Simonyi Survey Telescope had observed ED138 some 30 times between March 4 and July 11. Then, using the Asteroid Institute's ADAM Precovery tool, which combs archives for old matches to newly found objects, the team saw it again in 2016 images taken with the Dark Energy Camera at another observatory in Chile. Combined, the data allowed astronomers to trace the object's path over a decade. 

The Vera C. Rubin Observatory took these images of 2026 ED138, a possible dwarf planet beyond Pluto that may be one of the largest and brightest objects ever found that far from the sun. Credit: NSF–DOE Vera C. Rubin Observatory / NOIRLab / SLAC / AURA / M. Jurić / D. de Martin / M. Zamani

For now, scientists call ED138 a dwarf planet candidate because they still need to prove it's round. Icy objects of this size usually get pulled into a round shape by their own gravity. To be sure, though, scientists need to observe it with other telescopes in infrared light — a kind of light our eyes can't see — to pin down its exact size and other details.

Distant objects like ED138 are artifacts from the era when the planets, including Earth, formed, and they could help explain how the solar system and its giant planets came together.

Over the next 10 years, Rubin will photograph the whole southern sky repeatedly. Scientists believe this new detection is just the beginning of many discoveries to come. 

"Any others hiding in the places earlier surveys skipped are now within reach," Holman said.

Moon phase today: What the Moon will look like on October 29, 2026

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

The full Moon may be behind us, but there's still plenty of lunar activity to look forward to as October draws to a close.

What is today's Moon phase?

As of Thursday, Oct. 29, NASA's Daily Moon Guide tracker tells us the Moon is in the Waning Gibbous phase, with 87% of its surface visible.

Tonight, without the use of visual aids you should be able to spot the Tycho Crater, Copernicus Crater, and the Mare Imbrium. Binoculars will also help you see the Posidonius Crater, the Mare Humorum, and the Grimaldi Basin. Plus, if you have a telescope, you'll also see the Apollo 12 landing spot, the Rupes Altai, and the Caucasus Mountains.

When is the next Full Moon?

The next Full Moon will take place on November 24.

What are Moon phases?

The Moon takes approximately 29.5 days to complete an orbit around Earth, going through a series of phases along the way. Depending on its position relative to Earth and the Sun, the amount of its illuminated surface we can see changes, creating the familiar sequence of crescents, quarter moons and the Full Moon. Once the cycle is complete, the pattern repeats, as explained by NASA.

The eight phases are:

New Moon - The Moon is between Earth and the sun, so the side we see is dark (in other words, it's invisible to the eye).

Waxing Crescent - A small sliver of light appears on the right side (Northern Hemisphere).

First Quarter - Half of the Moon is lit on the right side. It looks like a half-Moon.

Waxing Gibbous - More than half is lit up, but it's not quite full yet.

Full Moon - The whole face of the Moon is illuminated and fully visible.

Waning Gibbous - The Moon starts losing light on the right side. (Northern Hemisphere)

Third Quarter (or Last Quarter) - Another half-Moon, but now the left side is lit.

Waning Crescent - A thin sliver of light remains on the left side before going dark again.

ULA's Vulcan rocket is set to return to flight on Friday the 13th

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

United Launch Alliance announced Wednesday it plans to return its Vulcan rocket to service on November 13, nine months after a solid rocket booster anomaly threw the company's 2026 launch schedule into oblivion.

The return-to-flight mission will launch 40 satellites for the Amazon Leo broadband constellation, the first of 38 Vulcan flights Amazon has booked to deploy the bulk of its network. The mission will lift off from Cape Canaveral Space Force Station, Florida, and ULA said the launch date is pending range approval.

The launch is critical for ULA after its new flagship rocket suffered nozzle failures on its solid rocket boosters on two of its first four missions, most recently during a flight in February. In both cases, the rocket overcame the nozzle failure and completed its mission, but officials at the US Space Force, ULA's primary customer, lost confidence in Vulcan.
