Worms mate in man's eyes, get plucked out right before larvae burst out

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

After two months of discomfort, a man in South Korea went to an eye clinic where doctors pulled 11 living, wriggling worms from his eyes. They apparently made the most of their time on the man's peepers; at the time of extraction, many of the female worms were pregnant. The man narrowly avoided having them spew their loads of living larvae directly into his eyes.

The man in this case was a 71-year-old crop farmer. He told doctors that for the previous two months he had the sensation of having something stuck in both his eyes, which were also red and blurry. When ophthalmologists took a look, they quickly saw the problem: multiple mobile, whitish roundworms. His eyes were not in good shape either, as one might expect. The blood vessels in the membrane over the eye, the conjunctiva, were enlarged, making his eyes look red. And the cells of his cornea—the transparent, dome-shaped outer layer of the eye—had eroded, potentially after the helminths' hanky-panky.

According to a case report published in the New England Journal of Medicine, the doctors gave the man a topical treatment to numb his eyes and then began plucking out the worms one by one with fine forceps. They collected 11 adult worms, which ranged in size from 11 to 22 mm.

Meet the real-life Scottish surgeon who inspired Sherlock Holmes

📅 Oct 26, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

Sir Arthur Conan Doyle created one of the most beloved fictional detectives of all time in Sherlock Holmes, spawning countless novels, films, TV series, and so forth featuring the sleuth. Far fewer people know about the real-life inspiration for Holmes: a renowned surgeon at Scotland's University of Edinburgh named Joseph Bell, who taught Doyle when the author was still a young medical student. Bell is the subject of a new, eminently readable biography, Sleuth-Hound: The Case of the Real Sherlock Holmes, by medical historian Lindsey Fitzharris.

A noted science communicator with a fondness for the medically macabre, Fitzharris published a biography of Scottish surgical pioneer Joseph Lister, The Butchering Art, in 2017—a great, if occasionally grisly, read. She followed up with 2022’s  The Facemaker: A Visionary Surgeon’s Battle to Mend the Disfigured Soldiers of World War I, about a WWI surgeon named Harold Gillies who rebuilt the faces of injured soldiers.

In 2020, Fitzharris hosted a documentary for the Smithsonian Channel, The Curious Life and Death Of…, exploring famous deaths, ranging from drug lord Pablo Escobar to magician Harry Houdini. Fitzharris performed virtual autopsies, experimented with blood samples, interviewed witnesses, and conducted real-time demonstrations in hopes of gleaning fresh insights. And last year she and her husband, caricaturist Adrian Teal, collaborated on an illustrated children's book, Dead Ends! Flukes, Flops, and Failures that Sparked Medical Marvels.

Miranda Lambert Headlining Dallas Cowboys Thanksgiving Game Halftime Show

📅 Oct 25, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Texas native Miranda Lambert is set to headline the Dallas Cowboys’ halftime show when the team plays the Philadelphia Eagles on Thanksgiving Day at AT&T Stadium.

Related

The game airs on Nov. 26 on Fox, starting at 3:30 p.m. CT.

The annual Cowboys halftime entertainment segment, officially titled the Salvation Army Red Kettle Kickoff Halftime Show, puts a spotlight on the needs of millions during the holiday season and beyond — with the Red Kettle Campaign raising more than $3.4 billion over the past three decades. Donations provide assistance to those in need in the community in which they’re given. Information about giving help or getting help is available at SalvationArmyUSA.org.

“It’s the 30th anniversary. You’ve gotta take the bull by the horns,” Lambert — who released her 10th solo studio album,Crisco, in early October — says in the promo spot seen below that was released Sunday (Oct. 25), riding in to AT&T Stadium and revealing the halftime performance news.

In a press statement also released on Sunday, Lambert adds, “There’s something special about how music brings people together, especially when it’s to spread joy and hope to families who need it most. The Salvation Army and the Dallas Cowboys have built an incredible tradition with the Red Kettle Kickoff, and I can’t wait to be back home in Texas this Thanksgiving to help carry it forward.”

Lambert’s Crisco album debuted at No. 4 on Billboard’s Top Country Albums following its Oct. 2 release, continuing the singer’s unbroken run of 11 top 10s on the chart.

“The Red Kettle Kickoff has become one of the Dallas Cowboys’ proudest traditions and one my family and our entire organization look forward to every Thanksgiving,” says Charlotte Jones, chief brand officer/co-owner of the Dallas Cowboys and former national advisory board chairman for The Salvation Army. “For 30 years, incredible artists have appeared on our halftime stage to help The Salvation Army bring hope to millions of people in need. We’re grateful to Miranda Lambert for joining that legacy and helping us continue this meaningful tradition.”

Past performers to headline the team’s Thanksgiving game halftime show include the late Dolly Parton, who memorably came out on the stadium field in a Cowboys cheerleaders outfit in 2023. Reba McEntire was the inaugural star to headline the show in 1997. Over the years, Destiny’s Child, Carrie Underwood, the Jonas Brothers, Selena Gomez, Lainey Wilson and Post Malone were also among the artists leading the halftime entertainment for the Cowboys.

Last week it was announced that U2 will perform at the NFL’s first-ever Thanksgiving Eve football game — when the Green Bay Packers play the Los Angeles Rams at SoFi Stadium in Inglewood, Calif.
