The Amazing Digital Circus made a surprise cameo in a recent episode of Law & Order Toronto: Criminal Intent. Unfortunately, the writers behind this iteration of the long-running police-procedural drama don’t seem to think much of the hit indie animated series.

In the episode, titled “The Business of Boys,” the Toronto Major Crimes Unit investigates a video depicting the gruesome murder of a local school teacher. As the detectives interrogate the student they believe filmed the video, they note his interest in cosplaying The Amazing Digital Circus‘ resident rascally rabbit, Jax, and suggest he wore the costume to hide his face during the crime.

In fairness, the detectives’ conclusion rests on sound evidence (the kid accidentally recorded a reflection of his mask while filming the video), but the scene frames the boy’s interest in The Amazing Digital Circus as all the proof they need of his murderous potential. As the detective swipes through the many in-costume selfies the student took, his voice and body language suggest he sees the kid’s interest in the animated series as a big red flag all by itself.

As wild as it is to find out that The Amazing Digital Circus is canon in the Law & Order universe, the fact that the show paints one of its fans as a sadistic assistant to murder takes all the wind out of the reveal’s sails. And this isn’t the first time Law and Order has done something like this.

Throughout its decades-long run, Law & Order has never been very kind to nerd culture. If a film, book, or video game plays a big role in an episode, it’s usually what caused whatever crime the detectives are solving. Law & Order: Special Victims Unit, in particular, really loves to pick on video games, with entire episodes centered on fictionalized versions of Grand Theft Auto and Slender Man that drove their fans to murderous madness.

Even episodes not centered on a piece of pop culture don’t miss a chance to pick on “over-enthusiastic” fans of whatever’s popular right now. Whether they’re the perp behind the week’s crime, a red herring who leads the detectives astray, or a naive victim, Law & Order‘s geeks are always pitifully awkward at best and “How do you function?” incompetent at worst.

In an age when geek culture is more popular and accepted than ever, it’s depressing to see a show as popular as Law & Order so often sink to the classic “Aw, aren’t nerds weird?” mentality. By now, it’s pretty low-hanging fruit, and you’d think a series about the complexity of the human condition would have found a better way to explore our relationship with pop culture by now.

Taylor Swift’s ‘The Life of a Showgirl’ Spends 14th Week Atop Billboard 200

📅 Oct 11, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Taylor Swift’s The Life of a Showgirl spends a 14th nonconsecutive week atop the Billboard 200 chart (dated Oct. 17), earning 93,000 equivalent album units in the United States in the week ending Oct. 8 (down 46%), according to Luminate. The set jumped back to the top of the list a week ago, vaulting 21-1, after it was reissued in a deluxe edition on Sept. 25 with four previously unreleased songs, including the Billboard Hot 100-topper “Patient Zero.”

With a 14th week in the lead for The Life of a Showgirl, Swift adds a milestone 100th career week at No. 1 on the chart across her 15 No. 1s, extending her record among soloists. Elvis Presley has the second-most weeks at No. 1 among soloists, with 67. The total encompasses her 15 No. 1 albums; she is tied with Drake for the most among soloists. Only The Beatles have more weeks at No. 1 (132) and more No. 1 albums (19) among all artists. The Billboard 200 began publishing on a regular, weekly basis in March 1956.

Also in the top 10 of the latest Billboard 200, Drake’s HABIBTI soars 116-2 (returning to its peak) after it was reissued with four additional songs. The Beatles’ chart-topping Rubber Soul is back on the chart for the first time in more than a decade after an expansive deluxe reissue, as it re-enters at No. 4. Plus, Mac Miller’s The Divine Feminine returns to the top 10, vaulting 159-10, after its 10th anniversary reissue.

The Billboard 200 chart ranks the most popular albums of the week in the U.S. based on multi-metric consumption as measured in equivalent album units, compiled by Luminate. Units comprise album sales, track equivalent albums (TEA) and streaming equivalent albums (SEA). Each unit equals one album sale, or 10 individual tracks sold from an album, or 2,500 ad-supported or 1,000 paid/subscription on-demand official audio and video streams generated by songs from an album. The new Oct. 17, 2026-dated chart will be posted in full on Billboard‘s website on Oct. 13. For all chart news, follow @billboard and @billboardcharts on both X and Instagram.

Of The Life of a Showgirl’s 93,000 equivalent album units earned in the latest tracking week, SEA units comprise 65,000 (down 52%, equaling 66.64 million on-demand official streams of the set’s tracks; it falls 1-2 on Top Streaming Albums), album sales comprise 27,000 (up 28%; it moves 5-2 on Top Album Sales) and TEA units comprise the remaining sum (down 90%).

Drake’s HABIBTI returns to its peak position, as it flies 116-2 after it was reissued with four additional songs on Oct. 2. The set earned 71,000 equivalent album units in the latest tracking week (up 506%), with nearly 70,000 of that sum driven by SEA activity (up 497%). The expanded edition of the album, dubbed HABIBTI (FOMO), includes the added tracks “Solar Eclipse” (with Don Toliver), “Quebec,” “Cold Shoulder” (with Toliver and Yebba) and “Classic PT2.” All versions of the album are combined for tracking and charting purposes.

Olivia Rodrigo’s chart-topping you seem pretty sad for a girl so in love falls a spot to No. 3 on the latest Billboard 200 with 66,000 equivalent album units earned (down 2%).

The Beatles’ former No. 1 Rubber Soul returns to the Billboard 200, re-entering at No. 4 after its expansive reissue on Oct. 2 across an array of formats and editions. The album, which topped the chart for six weeks in 1966, has been absent from the ranking for more than a decade. It was last on the chart dated Jan. 16, 2016, and last in the top 10 on the April 9, 1966-dated chart. The album includes familiar favorites like “Norwegian Wood (This Bird Has Flown),” “Michelle” and “In My Life.” In the latest tracking week, Rubber Soul earned 58,000 equivalent album units (up 1,626%). Of that sum, album sales comprise 50,000 (up from a negligible sum in the week previous, it reenters at No. 1 on Top Album Sales), SEA units comprise 7,000 (equaling 7.4 million on-demand official streams of the set’s tracks; up 144%) and TEA units comprise the remainder (up 691%).

With 58,000 units earned, and 50,000 copies sold, Rubber Soul lands the band its best week by units and pure sales in seven years. The Fab Four last notched a larger week by units and sales when Abbey Road garnered 81,000 units, with 70,000 of that in pure sales, on the Oct. 12, 2019-dated chart following its similar deluxe reissue.

With Rubber Soul re-entering at No. 4 on the Billboard 200, The Beatles score their highest rank on the chart since the Nov. 12, 2022-dated list, when Revolver reentered at No. 4 after its own deluxe reissue.

Notably, with Swift (No. 1), Drake (No. 2) and The Beatles (No. 4) all in the top five together on the new Billboard 200 chart, it’s the first time the three superstar acts have shared space in the top five — or even in the top 10 — in the same week. The trio of acts has the most No. 1 albums among all acts. The Beatles lead with 19, while Drake and Swift are tied with 15 each.

Three former No. 1s are next up on the latest Billboard 200. Morgan Wallen’s The the Problem falls 4-5 (55,000 equivalent album units earned, down 2%); Ella Langley’s Dandelion dips 3-6 (54,000, down 7%); and Noah Kahan’s The Great Divide drops 5-7 (46,000, up 2%). Olivia Dean’s The Art of Loving holds at No. 8 (35,000, down 1%) and Kahan’s Stick Season is a non-mover at No. 9 (33,000, up less than 1%).

Closing out the latest top 10 is Mac Miller’s The Divine Feminine, which rises 159-10 after it was reissued on Oct. 2 for its 10th anniversary with three additional tracks. The set debuted and peaked at No. 2 on the Oct. 8, 2016-dated chart. In the latest tracking week, The Divine Feminine earned 32,000 equivalent album units (up 218%). Of that sum, album sales comprise 18,000 (up 2,995%, it reenters at No. 4 on Top Album Sales), SEA units comprise 14,000 (equaling 14.68 million official on-demand streams of the set’s songs) and TEA units comprise a negligible sum.

Sonic the Hedgehog is turning thirty-five, and Sega is honoring the Blue Blur’s mid-life milestone in style with Sonic the Hedgehog: Memories and Beyond, an epic animated short celebrating the franchise’s history and future.

Premiering November 12, 2026, the short follows Sonic, Tails, Knuckles, Amy, Shadow, and Rouge as they try to stop Dr. Eggman from upgrading Metal Sonic into the ultimate weapon. This premise might sound like standard Sonic fare at first, but the short’s new teaser makes it clear Sonic and the gang won’t have an easy time.

If this short’s title and preview are any indication, memories will play a key role in this seminal Sonic adventure, with Dr. Eggman seemingly infusing Metal Sonic with the power of Sonic’s own memories. Suffice it to say, facing off against the nostalgia-powered Metal will force Sonic and his friends to reflect on their shared history, complete with mid-battle flashbacks that will surely imbue them with the raw power of friendship à la Dragon Ball.

Centering Sonic’s thirty-first birthday celebration on the franchise’s in-world history is a pretty obvious choice, but I doubt the short will be just a look back at the Blue Blur’s past. And it really shouldn’t be.

As Sonic fans already know well, change is one of the franchise’s only constants. Since his debut on the Sega Genesis, Sonic has been on one heck of a journey as a video game mascot, plummeting to deep lows before rocketing back to impressive heights. But only recently has the series finally acknowledged how much it’s changed since the good old days of sixteen-bit processing.

Ever since long-time comics author Ian Flynn took the reins as the series’ de facto chief narrative developer, Sonic the Hedgehog has consistently rejected the crippling nostalgia addiction that has claimed so many other franchises. The last major Sonic game, Sonic Frontiers, highlighted how much Sonic’s relationship with his closest friend has changed over the years. This shift towards a more introspective mindset has also carried into other Sonic media, including spin-offs like Memories and Beyond.

By pitting Sonic and friends against a foe powered by memories of the way things used to be, Memories and Beyond is, in a roundabout way, putting the franchise’s past and future on a collision course. While the short clearly aims to honor the series’ history with some satisfying fan service, it’s also obvious it will embrace the forward momentum that’s defined the series’ latest entries and push Sonic and pals into the future.

📅 Oct 09, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

A Call of Duty blog dropped a treasure trove of information for Modern Warfare 4‘s DMZ mode today, including good news for the more eager players. The mode is getting an early-access window starting on Oct. 20, exclusive to those who acquired the game ahead of time.

Modern Warfare 4‘s full release is slated for Friday, Oct. 23, but DMZ is opening its gates three days early if you preordered. The campaign is also available early, on Oct. 16, if you own any edition of the game.

Image via Activision

DMZ is evolving drastically in scope from its old beta into “Call of Duty’s definitive extraction shooter experience,” by the developer’s admission. This pushes it fully into the burgeoning genre, with its own base-building, progression, and even a bounty system for players.

The blog post dove into the Forward Operating Base (FOB), your respite between incursions in Hajin. Here, you’ll manage operators, craft or improve weapons, find vendors, and do general housekeeping when you’re not in a sortie.

It also showcased the deep operator progression, including operator levels, prestige bonuses, and three skill trees. The game has an MIA system for recovering lost agents using DMZ cash, which reminds me of Rainbow Six Extraction (and I might be the only one who remembers it).

Opening up early access to a higher-stakes, punishing extraction-based game mode is bound to draw criticism from parts of the fanbase, however. The three days of extra progression could provide an advantage over players dropping in on release, and that asymmetry would be more troublesome in the extraction mode than in multiplayer or Warzone.

As someone who played the old DMZ with my friends almost every night, there seems to be enough depth to give it some longevity, especially if it gets regular updates and tweaks every few months. With Battlefield 6‘s season five launching on Oct. 13, I think I might have to ask my boss for time off for “an early Halloween.”

Ubisoft is shutting down this forgotten Rainbow Six game you probably didn’t even know existed

📅 Oct 08, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

The Rainbow Six franchise has a wealth of installments, and when it comes to this amount of volume, you can’t always get them right. That’s the case for Rainbow Six Mobile, which is shutting down in January 2027, Ubisoft announced today.

The game will stay up until Jan. 15, 2027, the developer said, and its upcoming season “will be released as planned next week.” The announcement teases more updates to prepare for the transition, though it’s unclear what this means in practice.

Rainbow Six Mobile has had an unusual run since its reveal in 2022. At the time, Ubisoft said it was a version of Rainbow Six Siege built from the ground up to “adapt the Siege experience to mobile devices.”

Rainbow Six Extraction, the other forgotten R6 spinoff. Image via Ubisoft

Despite a few closed tests and soft launches after 2023 and a litany of changes in 2024, the game only released globally in February 2026. It has had less than eight months in the hands of players all over the world before being sunset—which, as we all know by now, is the “your dog went to live on a farm” of the gaming industry.

At first glance, Rainbow Six Mobile may almost seem doomed to fail. Siege‘s identity is built on its tactical, unforgiving gameplay—the kind that may get you to hold an angle with an ACOG on your gun, so tensely you can barely breathe. It’s a hard element to bring to a mobile game.

“Our goal is not to have you stop playing on PC or console and switch to mobile,” the team said on the announcement blog post. “We know there is nothing like the adrenaline rush of a close Siege match on the big screen or the precision of a mouse and keyboard.” Instead, the game aimed to let fans “get the R6 experience” in “short, accessible bursts,” the developers said.

It’s unclear what will happen to Rainbow Six Mobile until its shutdown date, but Ubisoft will announce more of its plans “in the coming weeks,” according to its announcement.

Suno Hires Ticketmaster Executive to Lead New Live Experiences Division

Suno has hired Michael Chua to be its vp and general manager of Suno Live Experiences. As part of his job, Chua will be overseeing the operations of Songkick, the concert discovery platform which Suno acquired as part of its partnership with Warner Music Group last November.

According to Suno spokesperson, Chua’s team will “identify new ways to bring artists closer to their fans and help fans discover and connect with their favorite artists.” This includes “improving concert recommendations.” Particularly, Suno’s Chief Music Officer, Paul Sinclair, adds that a “central focus” for their live team will be “helping emerging artists, like those in our Spark program.” Spark, launched in June, offers grants, marketing support and access to Suno’s songwriting camps for young users of the platform.

Prior to taking this newly created post at Suno, Chua was the vp of global business development and strategic partnerships at Ticketmaster, a division of Live Nation Entertainment. As part of his role there, he organized product partnerships with Google, YouTube, Spotify, Apple Music, TikTok, Snap and more, helping Ticketmaster drive more ticket sales even outside of the Ticketmaster app. Most recently, Ticketmaster and Live Nation facilitated Spotify’s new Reserved program, which launched in May and identifies an artist’s most dedicated fans on the platform then holds two tickets for those fans when the artist announces a tour. 

The move is part of Suno’s strategy to build “the first creative entertainment platform,” as Sinclair puts it. “Live music is a pivotal part of that.” On top of its AI music models, which can create songs from text, visual or audio inputs, the company also boasts a streaming arm with dedicated editorial curators and a short form video feed, similar to Tiktok, to get music makers interacting with each other’s work.

News of Chua’s appointment comes about a month after Suno’s biggest model launch to date — v6, a suite of AI music models that were its first made “in partnership with the music industry,” as the company put it. Specifically, these models were made with support from Warner Music Group, BMG and Believe, all of which Suno struck partnerships with before v6’s launch. In exchange for a revenue share from v6, the three music companies provided music to help train the Suno model.

While this was intended to be a step towards reconciliation with the music industry establishment, v6 prompted Universal Music Group and Sony Music, two of the major music companies, to file their second copyright infringement lawsuit against Suno. The two claimed that “v6 is not a fresh start,” as the labels’ lawyers put it. “It is the fruit of the same poisoned tree. Suno’s development of v6 continued, rather than ended, its unauthorized exploitation of plaintiffs’ copyrighted recordings,” citing concerns about possible “synthetic data” use to train the v6 models.

Chua said of his new role: “As Suno builds tools that help musicians create, reach fans and build lasting careers, live music has to be part of that. I see an incredible opportunity at Suno to help artists take a song from the first idea to the stage and bring their fans along the whole way. Bringing Suno and Songkick together opens up new possibilities to better connect artists and fans and ultimately create entirely new experiences that couldn’t have existed before Suno’s industry-leading tools.”

“We’re building the first creative entertainment platform, and live music is a pivotal part of that,” Sinclair’s full quote reads. “A central focus of our live experiences team will be helping emerging artists, like those in our Spark program, an incubator program designed to help independent artists bring their music projects to life and more easily create live experiences. Michael has spent his career at the intersection of tech and live music and I’m excited to build this team with his support.” 

F.B.I. Director Kash Patel Engaged to Country Singer Girlfriend Alexis Wilkins: ‘Went and Got the Law Involved’

📅 Oct 07, 2026  |  Category: Politics  |  Read Source →

F.B.I. director Kash Patel popped the question to his country singer girlfriend, Alexis Wilkins, over the weekend. In an Instagram post on Sunday (Oct. 4), Wilkins proudly announced the news in a post in which the couple both smiled as Wilkins held up her engagement ring. “Went and got the law involved 💍❤️,” she cheekily captioned the pic.

According to The New York Times, Patel, 46, and Wilkins, 27, met at a party in fall 2022 and at country singer John Rich’s Nashville home, where a publicist had brought along Wilkins. The paper reported that Patel complimented Wilkins’ jacket that night and by early 2023 the couple were dating. Wilkins was also in the gallery during Patel’s combative Senate confirmation hearing, with Wilkins saying she was attracted to Patel’s honesty and his “incredible” values and character during an interview on The Megyn Kelly Show last July.

Patel’s relationship with Wilkins — who signed with WME and artist management company the AMG in 2020 — has come under scrutiny over the past year over claims that the nation’s top law enforcement agent had used taxpayer funds to pay for his trips to see Wilkins perform, as well as for the couple’s date nights. The Times also reported that the F.B.I. had dispatched tactical agents to drive the singer to hair appointments and to stand guard at a senior center in Dixon, Ill. where she sang for a small group of young conservatives.

Though SWAT teams are typically trained to arrest violent criminals, free hostages and break up terror cells, former F.B.I. agents told the paper that Patel’s reported demand that a rotating group of SWAT officers provide security for Wilkins during her gigs, personal appearances and errands is unprecedented. An F.B.I. spokesperson told the Times that the full-time security detail for the civilian romantic partner of a federal official was necessary due to a reported “hundreds” of death threats aimed at the singer; the paper noted that the spouses of cabinet and congressional officials do not routinely receive full-time government-funded security details.

To date, Wilkins, whose LinkedIn says she’s been a recording artist, performer and songwriter since August 2007 (when she would have been eight years old), has not released a full-length album, but has issued the six-song 2023 EP Grit, featuring the songs “Quite Like Whiskey,” “Heaven In a Stetson” and “Half as Cowboy,” and a handful of singles.
